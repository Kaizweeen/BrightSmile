# DentaSync part 1: Foundation and scheduling

- **Date:** 2026-09-29
- **Status:** Approved in brainstorming on 2026-09-29, section by section; this document waits for Kai's review
- **Scope:** Part 1 of 5 (see the roadmap in section 2): accounts and roles, branches and chairs, dentist schedules, procedures with turnover buffers, the appointment lifecycle with conflict checks, the chair calendar, patient records, and the Philippine Dental Association (PDA) dental chart.

DentaSync runs one dental practice with three branches in the Philippines. The branch names Downtown, Westside, and Metro North are placeholders until the practice gives its own. Staff get accounts by scanning a QR code kept at their branch, and the owner or a manager approves them. The front desk books each patient with a dentist and a chair, and the database makes a double booking impossible: across branches, and including the time a chair needs for sanitizing after a visit. Dentists see their day across branches and keep each patient's record on the PDA dental chart. The owner sees every branch at once.

## 2. Roadmap

Each part gets its own spec, plan, and build.

1. **Foundation and scheduling (this spec).**
2. **Inventory:** a practice-wide catalog, stock per branch in lots with expiry dates, receiving, transfers between branches (requested, approved, in transit, received), reorder points per branch with Critical, Low, and Normal badges, purchase orders, and deducting each procedure's materials when a visit is completed.
3. **Patient portal:** patient logins, booking requests (they enter as Requested), upcoming visits, the full PDA medical history intake, and email confirmations.
4. **Analytics:** chair utilization per branch, daily volume, no-show rates, stock turnover, items expiring within 30, 60, and 90 days, and consumption cost per branch.
5. **SMS:** confirmations and reminders.

**Where the original prompt's deliverables land:** the branch switcher and the chair calendar are in part 1 and the inventory table in part 2; the part 1 tables are in section 7, with part 2's sketched in section 19; the booking validation and chair conflict routes are in part 1 (section 11) and the stock transfer routes in part 2.

## 3. Goals and success criteria

1. A dentist can never hold two overlapping visits, at any branch, and a chair can never be booked while it is still in turnover. Enforced by the database and proven by tests.
2. A booking for a dentist at one branch while their schedule places them at another is refused. Proven by a test.
3. Every role sees and does exactly what section 5 allows, checked on the server for every route and page, and proven by a test per role.
4. A new staff member is working within 5 minutes of scanning the branch QR, once someone approves them.
5. The front desk books a returning patient in under a minute: find them, pick procedures, pick a time, save.
6. Every status change follows the lifecycle in section 8.6, enforced by the database.
7. Every view of a patient record and every change to one is in the audit log.
8. Building and testing need no paid account and no installed database.

## 4. Architecture

- **App:** Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript), React 19, Tailwind CSS 4, shadcn/ui components, TanStack Query 5 for data the browser fetches and changes, and Zod 4 for every input.
- **API:** REST route handlers under `/api/v1`. Pages render on the server by calling the same service functions the routes call; client components read and change data through the routes.
- **Database:** Postgres 17 through Drizzle ORM. Migrations are SQL files generated by drizzle-kit, plus hand-written SQL for what Drizzle cannot express (the exclusion constraints and the triggers). Development and tests run PGlite in process: a data folder for development, and a fresh in-memory database per test file. Production uses node-postgres against Neon in Singapore, or any Postgres 17. One module picks the driver from `DATABASE_URL`. PGlite is pinned to the same Postgres major version as production.
- **Auth:** Better Auth 1.7 with its username plugin and sessions stored in Postgres. Its own sign-up endpoint is turned off: accounts are created only by the join route (6.3) and `/setup` (6.1). Better Auth requires an email on every user, so each account gets a random address under the reserved `.invalid` domain, which can never receive mail; it is never shown or used. Telemetry is off.
- **Authorization:** one guard, `authorize(user, action, branch)`, called by every route handler and every page before touching data. It reads the person's current role, branches, and status from the database on every request. The permission table (section 5) lives in one file and is unit tested.
- **Integrity:** the rules that must never break live in Postgres: exclusion constraints for overlaps, triggers for the lifecycle and for records that are never edited, foreign keys that keep chairs inside their branch, and check constraints for values.
- **Time:** stored as `timestamptz`. Every day boundary, schedule block, and display uses Asia/Manila (UTC+8, no daylight saving) through `Intl`, never the server's time zone.
- **QR codes:** the `qrcode` package draws them as SVG. Phones scan them with their own camera app.

## 5. Roles and permissions

Roles are **owner** (the prompt's Super Admin / Practice Owner), **manager** (Branch Manager / Front Desk), and **dentist** (Dentist / Hygienist, with a title such as Dentist, Orthodontist, or Hygienist). The patient role arrives with the portal in part 3. A practice has exactly one owner.

A person covers branches: the owner covers all of them; managers and dentists cover the branches listed for them. A person who **sees patients** can be booked, has a schedule, and writes clinical records. Every dentist sees patients, and the owner can turn it on for their own account, since practice owners are often dentists.

| Area | Owner | Manager | Dentist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar | All branches | Their branches | Their branches, plus their own visits at any branch |
| Book, move, confirm, check in, mark no-show, cancel | Yes | Their branches | No |
| Start treatment, complete | Yes | Their branches | Their own visits |
| Patients: find, view, add, edit details | Yes | Yes, practice-wide | View practice-wide; edit allergies and medical alerts |
| Tooth chart, exams, treatment notes: read | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Tooth chart, exams, treatment notes: write | When they see patients | No | Their own visits |
| Dentist schedules and time off | Yes | Their branches | Read their own |
| Join requests | Approve for any branch | Approve for their branches; may grant only manager or dentist, and only branches they cover | No |
| Staff: role, branches, title, disable, reset QR | Everyone but themselves | Non-owner staff of their branches | No |
| Branches, chairs, procedures, branch QR codes, practice name | Yes | No | No |
| All branches view | Yes | When they cover 2 or more branches | No |
| Access log | Yes | No | No |

Every "no" is enforced by the guard on the server; the interface also hides what a person cannot use.

## 6. Accounts by QR

### 6.1 First run (`/setup`)

`/setup` works only while there is no owner. It asks for the setup code (the `SETUP_TOKEN` environment variable, at least 32 random characters, compared in constant time), the practice name, and the owner's full name, username, and password. It creates the practice and the owner, signs the owner in, and opens Settings so they can add the branches. Once an owner exists, `/setup` answers 404; only `/setup/recover` (6.6) remains.

### 6.2 The branch QR

- Each branch has a join code of 22 random URL-safe characters (about 128 bits), stored as is: on its own it grants nothing (6.3). The QR encodes `{APP_URL}/join/{code}`.
- Settings, Branches, "Print QR poster" opens a printable A4 page: the practice and branch names, the QR, and "Staff only. Scan to ask for a DentaSync account."
- "Replace QR" (owner) makes a new code at once; the printed old one stops working. Requests already made with it stay open.

### 6.3 Joining (`/join/{code}`)

- An unknown or replaced code shows "This QR code no longer works. Ask the owner for the current one."
- Otherwise the page names the practice and branch and asks for: full name (2 to 80 characters), username (3 to 30: lowercase letters, digits, dot, and underscore; unique regardless of case), password (10 to 128 characters, typed twice), and "I work here as": Front desk, or Dentist or hygienist.
- Sending it creates a **pending** account with the requested role and branch, and signs the person in. Until someone approves them, every page shows only "Waiting for approval. Ask the owner or a manager at {branch} to approve you." and Sign out. No route answers a pending account.
- Limits: 5 requests an hour from one IP address; at most 20 open requests per branch. A request expires 7 days after it is made; expired pending accounts cannot sign in and are deleted the next time a join or approval screen loads.

### 6.4 Approving (Staff page)

- The owner and managers see the open requests they may approve: name, username, requested role, branch, and when it was made.
- **Approve:** confirm or change the role (manager or dentist) and the branches (the request's branch by default), and the title for a dentist. The account becomes active.
- **Decline:** the pending account is deleted.
- The audit log records who approved or declined whom.

### 6.5 Changing and disabling staff

- The owner can change anyone else's role (manager or dentist), branches, title, and "sees patients", and disable or re-enable them. A manager can do the same for non-owner staff of their branches, but cannot give a branch they do not cover or make anyone owner.
- Because the guard reads role, branches, and status on every request, a change takes effect on the person's next click. Disabling also deletes their sessions at once.
- Disabling is never blocked, so access can always end immediately. The Staff page then lists the disabled dentist's upcoming visits so they can be moved.
- A disabled person's name stays on everything they did.

### 6.6 Forgotten passwords

- The owner (for anyone else) or a manager (for non-owner staff of their branches) presses "Reset password". The screen shows a QR and a link to `/reset/{token}`, valid for 15 minutes and one use. The token is 32 random characters, stored only as its SHA-256 hash.
- The person scans it, sets a new password, and all their sessions end.
- The owner resets their own password at `/setup/recover` with the setup code.
- The login page says "Forgot your password? Ask your manager for a reset QR."

### 6.7 Sessions and signing in

- Username and password. A session lasts 12 hours from sign-in (one working day) and is not extended by activity.
- Cookies are httpOnly, SameSite=Lax, and Secure in production.
- Sign-in attempts are rate limited per IP address, using Better Auth's limiter with its counts stored in Postgres.

## 7. Data model

Part 1 creates these tables. Every table has `created_at`; tables without a natural key have `id uuid primary key`. Names follow the prompt's schema where they fit.

| Table | Columns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `practice` | name (1 to 80) | Exactly one row. |
| `branches` | code, name (1 to 40), address (up to 200), phone (up to 20), operating_hours, join_code, active, sort | `code` is used in URLs (`downtown`): 2 to 24 lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens, unique, never `all`. `operating_hours` is JSON: for each weekday (0 Sunday to 6 Saturday) either closed or one opening and closing time on the 15-minute grid, validated by Zod. The prompt's `chairs_count` is not stored: it is the count of the branch's active chairs, so it can never disagree with them. |
| `chairs` | branch_id, number (1 to 99), label (up to 30, such as Ortho or General), active | Primary key (branch_id, number). |
| `users` | Better Auth's table, plus username, role, status (pending, active, disabled), title, sees_patients, primary_branch_id, requested_branch_id, approved_by, approved_at | At most one owner (a partial unique index). |
| `user_branches` | user_id, branch_id | Primary key (user_id, branch_id). The branches a manager or dentist covers. |
| `dentist_schedules` | dentist_id, branch_id, day_of_week, start_time, end_time | `day_of_week` counts as in `operating_hours`. Times on the 15-minute grid, inside the branch's hours that day, at a branch the dentist covers. |
| `dentist_time_off` | dentist_id, starts_at, ends_at, reason (up to 100) | Leave, seminars. Adding time off over booked visits lists those visits; nothing is cancelled automatically. |
| `procedures` | name (1 to 60, unique), duration_minutes (5 to 480, a multiple of 5), buffer_minutes (0 to 120), active, sort | `buffer_minutes` is the chair's turnover time after the visit. |
| `patients` | chart_no, last_name, first_name, middle_name, birthday, sex, mobile, email, address, occupation, guardian_name, emergency_name, emergency_mobile, hmo_provider, hmo_member_no, insurance_effective, allergies, medical_alerts, consent_at, consent_by, home_branch_id, created_by, updated_at, updated_by | One record for the whole practice. `chart_no` is a number the database assigns, for paper references. Mobiles are stored as `+639XXXXXXXXX`. `allergies` holds the PDA list (local anesthetic, penicillin or antibiotics, sulfa drugs, aspirin, latex) plus free text for others. `medical_alerts` is free text up to 500 characters. `consent_at` and `consent_by` record when the patient signed the practice's data privacy consent and who recorded it. |
| `appointments` | patient_id, dentist_id, branch_id, chair_number, start_time, end_time, chair_free_at, status, source (staff, walk_in, later portal), note (up to 500), cancel_reason (up to 200), confirmed_at, checked_in_at, treatment_started_at, completed_at, cancelled_at, created_by, updated_at | `chair_free_at` is the end plus turnover. Foreign key (branch_id, chair_number) to `chairs`. |
| `appointment_procedures` | appointment_id, position, procedure_id, name, duration_minutes, buffer_minutes | The prompt's `service_type`, as a list, because one visit often has several procedures. Name and minutes are copied at booking, so history survives edits. Part 2 attaches materials here. |
| `treatment_notes` | appointment_id, patient_id, author_id, body (1 to 4,000), amends_id | Never updated or deleted; a correction is a new note that amends the old one. |
| `chart_entries` | patient_id, appointment_id, branch_id, tooth, surfaces, code, note (up to 300), author_id, voided_at, voided_by, void_reason | Never deleted; a mistake is voided with a reason and stays visible, struck through. `appointment_id` may be empty for charting outside a visit, such as copying a paper chart. |
| `exams` | appointment_id (unique), patient_id, author_id, findings, updated_at | One per visit. Editable until the visit is completed. |
| `audit_log` | id (bigint), at, user_id, action, entity, entity_id, branch_id, details | Never updated or deleted. `details` never holds health information. |

**Enforced by the database.** "Active" means requested, confirmed, checked in, or in treatment.

1. A dentist never has two overlapping active visits, at any branch: `exclude using gist (dentist_id with =, tstzrange(start_time, end_time) with &&) where (active)`.
2. A chair never holds two active visits whose time plus turnover overlap: `exclude using gist (branch_id with =, chair_number with =, tstzrange(start_time, chair_free_at) with &&) where (active)`.
3. A patient never has two overlapping active visits: the same shape on `patient_id`.
4. A dentist's schedule blocks never overlap on the same weekday, even at different branches: an exclusion constraint on (dentist_id, day_of_week, the block's time range).
5. Status changes follow section 8.6: a trigger refuses any other change, and on insert allows only requested, confirmed, or checked in.
6. An appointment's chair belongs to its branch, through the composite foreign key.
7. `treatment_notes`, `chart_entries` (except its three void columns, which can be set once), and `audit_log` refuse updates and deletes, through a trigger.
8. Checks: `start_time < end_time <= chair_free_at`, chart teeth are valid FDI numbers, and the void columns are either all empty or all set.

## 8. Scheduling rules

### 8.1 Time

Starts sit on a 15-minute grid in Manila time, except walk-ins, which start at the current minute. Each branch has opening hours per weekday. Each dentist has weekly blocks, each at one branch.

### 8.2 Length and turnover

A visit lasts the sum of its procedures' durations. Afterwards the chair is blocked for the longest turnover buffer among them. The turnover blocks the chair, not the dentist.

### 8.3 Hard stops (the booking is never saved)

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `dentist_overlap`, `chair_overlap`, `patient_overlap` | A clash with an active visit (the database refuses these even if the check is skipped). |
| `dentist_elsewhere` | Part of the visit falls in the dentist's schedule block at another branch. This is the cross-branch rule: while Dr. Reyes works Downtown 9:00 to 12:00, Westside cannot book them at 10:00. |
| `dentist_time_off` | The visit overlaps the dentist's time off. |
| `inactive` | The chair, dentist, branch, or a procedure is inactive, or the dentist does not see patients. |
| `past` | The start has passed (walk-ins excepted). |

### 8.4 Warnings (saved only after "Book anyway")

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `outside_dentist_hours` | Part of the visit falls outside all of the dentist's blocks (overtime), without touching another branch's block. |
| `outside_branch_hours` | Part of the visit falls outside the branch's hours, or the branch is closed that day. |

A save with warnings must carry `acknowledgeWarnings: true`; otherwise it is refused with the warnings listed.

### 8.5 Open times

For a date, branch, and set of procedures (and optionally a dentist and a patient), the open times are every start on the grid where:

- a dentist who sees patients has a block at this branch covering the whole visit, is not on time off, and has no active visit that overlaps;
- at least one active chair at the branch is free for the visit plus turnover;
- the patient, if given, has no active visit that overlaps;
- the start has not passed.

Each open time lists its dentists and their free chairs. The lowest-numbered free chair is picked, and staff can change it.

### 8.6 Lifecycle

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Requested | Confirmed, Cancelled |
| Confirmed | Checked-In (on the visit's Manila date), No-Show (after its start), Cancelled |
| Checked-In | In-Treatment, Cancelled (left before treatment), Confirmed (undo a check-in) |
| In-Treatment | Completed |
| No-Show | Checked-In (arrived late, on the same Manila date) |

- Completed and Cancelled are final.
- Staff bookings start as Confirmed, or as Requested while the patient still has to confirm. Walk-ins start as Checked-In.
- Cancelling needs a reason (1 to 200 characters).
- The trigger enforces the pairs and stamps the matching `*_at` column (clearing `checked_in_at` on an undo); the service enforces the date and time rules and writes the audit log row in the same transaction.
- Returning a No-Show to Checked-In makes the visit active again, so the database refuses it if the time was given to someone else.

### 8.7 Moving

A Requested or Confirmed visit can change its time, chair, dentist, and procedures, through the same checks as a new booking, and keeps its status. A Checked-In visit can change only its chair, at the same branch.

### 8.8 Two front desks at once

The database refuses the second of two clashing bookings, and that person sees which visit won, for example "Dr. Reyes was just booked at 10:00 at Westside." Calendars refresh every 30 seconds and after every change.

## 9. Clinical records

### 9.1 The dental chart

- **Layout:** the PDA Dental Record Chart, with FDI numbering and the patient's right on the viewer's left. From top to bottom: temporary teeth 55 to 51 and 61 to 65; permanent 18 to 11 and 21 to 28; permanent 48 to 41 and 31 to 38; temporary 85 to 81 and 71 to 75. Code boxes sit above the upper rows and below the lower rows, and each tooth is a circle of five surfaces.
- **Surfaces:** the centre is occlusal (O), shown as incisal (I) on incisors and canines (teeth numbered x1 to x3). The segment toward the chart's horizontal midline is lingual (L), palatal on upper teeth; the opposite one is buccal (B), labial on front teeth. The segment toward the vertical midline is mesial (M), and the opposite one is distal (D).
- **Legend**, as printed on the PDA chart:
  - Condition: ✓ present teeth, D decayed (caries indicated for filling), M missing due to caries, MO missing due to other causes, Im impacted tooth, Sp supernumerary tooth, Rf root fragment, Un unerupted.
  - Restorations and prosthetics: Am amalgam filling, Co composite filling, JC jacket crown, Ab abutment, Att attachment, P pontic, In inlay, Imp implant, S sealants, Rm removable denture.
  - Surgery: X extraction due to caries, XO extraction due to other causes.
- **Surface codes:** D, Am, Co, In, and S need at least one surface; every other code applies to the whole tooth.
- **Current state:** for each surface, the latest entry that is not voided wins; an entry for the whole tooth covers all its surfaces. The code boxes and the circles show this state, and each tooth's history lists every entry with date, dentist, branch, and visit.
- **Adding:** someone who may write clicks a tooth, picks a code (grouped as in the legend), picks surfaces when the code needs them, and may add a note. The entry is saved to the patient's visit with that dentist that is checked in or in treatment, or without a visit when there is none.
- **Voiding:** the author, or the owner when they see patients, voids an entry with a reason.

### 9.2 The exam

One per visit, holding the sections under the PDA chart. Each item is either unmarked, or marked with an optional detail of up to 60 characters (such as "16, 26" for periapical X-rays):

- Periodontal screening: gingivitis, early periodontitis, moderate periodontitis, advanced periodontitis.
- Occlusion: class (molar), overjet, overbite, midline deviation, crossbite.
- Appliances: orthodontic, stayplate, others.
- TMD: clenching, clicking, trismus, muscle spasm.
- X-rays taken: periapical (tooth numbers), panoramic, cephalometric, occlusal (upper or lower), others.

`findings` stores this as JSON validated by Zod. The exam can be edited until its visit is completed.

### 9.3 Treatment notes and visits

- Treatment notes are free text per visit, shown by date with dentist, branch, and chair. A correction is an amending note.
- The Visits tab follows the PDA treatment record, without the money columns: date, branch, chair, tooth numbers (from that visit's chart entries), procedures, dentist, status, and the patient's next active visit.

## 11. API

REST, JSON, under `/api/v1`, validated by Zod. The session comes from Better Auth's cookie. Every write checks that the request's `Origin` is the app's own and that the body is JSON. Better Auth's own endpoints (sign in, sign out, session) live at `/api/auth/*`.

### 11.1 Booking validation

`POST /api/v1/appointments/validate`

```json
{ "branch": "westside", "chairNumber": 2, "dentistId": "…", "patientId": "…",
  "start": "2026-10-05T10:00:00+08:00", "procedureIds": ["…", "…"],
  "walkIn": false, "excludeAppointmentId": null }
```

Answers `200` with:

```json
{ "ok": false,
  "end": "2026-10-05T11:30:00+08:00", "chairFreeAt": "2026-10-05T11:50:00+08:00",
  "errors": [{ "code": "dentist_elsewhere", "message": "Dr. Reyes works at Downtown until 12:00." }],
  "warnings": [],
  "conflicts": [{ "kind": "chair", "appointmentId": "…", "start": "…", "end": "…", "chairFreeAt": "…",
                  "branch": "westside", "chairNumber": 2, "patient": "Santos, Ana", "dentist": "Dr. Cruz" }] }
```

### 11.2 Chair conflict detection

`GET /api/v1/chairs/conflicts?branch=westside&chair=2&from=…&until=…&exclude=…` answers `200` with `{ "conflicts": [...] }`: every active visit on that chair whose time plus turnover overlaps `[from, until)`, where `until` is the new visit's end plus its turnover.

## 12. Errors

- Every error has one shape: `{ "error": { "code", "message", "fields"?, "conflicts"?, "warnings"? } }`.
- `400` invalid input (with `fields`), `401` signed out, `403` not allowed, `404` not found or not visible to this person, `409` a clash (with `conflicts`), `415` not JSON, `422` a broken rule or unacknowledged warnings, `429` rate limited.
- A database exclusion violation (SQLSTATE `23P01`) becomes a `409` naming the clash in words, such as "Chair 2 is in turnover until 10:45." A refused status change becomes a `422` such as "A cancelled visit cannot be checked in."
- Unexpected errors are logged with a request ID and no patient details, and the screen shows "Something went wrong" with that ID.
- In the browser, a failed change rolls back what the screen showed and says why.

## 13. Security and privacy (RA 10173)

- The practice is the personal information controller, and whoever hosts DentaSync is its processor. Patient health information is sensitive personal information.
- Access follows section 5, checked on the server. Front desk reads clinical records and never writes them.
- The audit log records sign-ins (including failed ones), every view of a patient record, every change, and every staff and QR action.
- Passwords of at least 10 characters, hashed by Better Auth. Sessions and cookies as in 6.7. Join codes grant nothing alone; reset tokens are stored hashed; the setup code lives only in the environment.
- Headers: a Content Security Policy, HSTS, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `Referrer-Policy: same-origin` (join and reset links carry tokens), and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. The whole app is kept out of search engines.
- Logs never contain patient details or tokens.
- Data stays in one region (Singapore), which the practice's privacy notice must state.
- Backups from go-live: nightly and encrypted (section 16).

## 14. Testing

All tests run offline, with no accounts and no installed database.

- **Unit (Vitest):** open times; the lifecycle table; hard stops versus warnings, including `dentist_elsewhere` against `outside_dentist_hours`; Manila day boundaries around midnight; the surface directions for each quadrant, so mesial always faces the midline; the chart's current state per surface; the permission table, every action for every role; the exam findings shape.
- **Database (Vitest on PGlite):** each exclusion constraint (the same dentist at two branches, a chair during turnover, the same patient twice); cancelled, completed, and no-show visits do not block time; the lifecycle trigger accepts every pair in 8.6 and refuses the rest; overlapping schedule blocks across branches are refused; notes, chart entries, and the audit log cannot be edited or deleted.
- **API (Vitest calling the route handlers on PGlite, one signed-in person per role):** a Downtown manager cannot book at Westside; a dentist cannot approve staff; front desk cannot write the chart; a disabled person is refused on their next request; a pending account reaches nothing; a clash answers `409` with its conflict; unacknowledged warnings answer `422`; a write from another origin is refused.
- **End to end (Playwright, one run):** setup, a QR join, approval, booking, check-in, start of treatment, charting a tooth, the exam, a note, and completion.

## 16. Hosting and go-live (decided at go-live)

- **Free:** Neon Free for Postgres in Singapore (0.5 GB, which holds years of a 3-branch practice's records; it sleeps after 5 idle minutes, so the first request after that waits a moment; only 6 hours of restore history) and Netlify Free for the app (commercial use allowed; a hard cap of 300 credits a month, 15 per production deploy, and the site pauses when they run out, so releases are batched about weekly).
- **Paid, if the caps bite:** Vercel Pro at $20 a month (Vercel's free plan forbids commercial use), and a paid Neon plan for a longer restore window. No code changes either way.
- **Backups:** a nightly `pg_dump`, encrypted with a key only the owner holds, stored away from the database host. One restore is tested before go-live.
- **Environment:** `DATABASE_URL`, `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, `APP_URL`, `SETUP_TOKEN`.
- **Before go-live:** the practice's real branch names, addresses, hours, and chairs; its procedure list with durations and turnover; its dentists confirm the PDA legend and exam items; its privacy notice and consent form (with a lawyer); the printed QR posters; and the owner's setup.
